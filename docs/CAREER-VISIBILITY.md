## 1. Plain answer first

You are not a typical candidate, and that is an asset only if a stranger can see the one idea that connects everything in under a minute. Today they cannot. The strongest thing you have is not a paper or a
system. It is a habit: **you check whether your own evidence is any good, and you say so when it is not.** The fleet showed it when READER-YIELD-1 found that the click counts the system proudly reported were
mostly its own probes, and when your own code showed your most-downloaded paper's trajectory does not follow from its equations. Very few people building AI systems do that. Lead with it.

**The through-line (use this everywhere):** *I make evidence trustworthy enough to act on. First for transportation and aging policy, now for AI-assisted research.* Federal research programs, a national
neighborhood index, data products, and an autonomous research system with an audit method are one career, not four.

## 2. Weaknesses and blind spots, ranked by how much they cost you

I am not softening these. Each has a fix, and the fix is mostly in your hands, not the fleet's.

1. **Volume reads as noise.** 262 papers in 30 days from an AI-assisted pipeline, 728 in the corpus, many marked "not independently peer-reviewed". A skeptical reader (a researcher, a hiring manager at a serious
   lab) sees the count first and discounts everything, including the three or four pieces that are good. Fix: the front door shows five selected works and nothing else; the rest is labelled pipeline output and
   moved behind a link. Never lead with a paper count.
2. **The About page claims a readership the fleet's own measurement does not support.** It says "about 400 requests a day". READER-YIELD-1 found about 6 engaged readers a day from outside the fleet (44 a week),
   with the rest automation. A reviewer who reads your own reader-yield work next to that line concludes you overstate. Fix: replace it with the engaged-reader figure and its caveat (item REACH-FOCUS-1.5).
3. **No independent validation.** No customer, no co-author outside the pipeline, no user of the libraries, no outside reproduction, no named reference who is not you. "Built and run alone" is impressive and, to an employer,
   unproven. Fix, in order of cost: one talk or workshop slot using the accepted ignorance-audit conference paper; three people you know who run `ignorance-audit` or `agentic-collapse` and say so in public; one
   pilot with a team that has a real problem. One outside voice outweighs 100 of your own pages.
4. **Fringe-adjacent material sits next to serious work.** Papers such as consciousness-driven collapse, and the earlier bio line "inventing nature-inspired quantum computers", signal speculation to a
   reader deciding in seconds whether you are rigorous. Fix: employer-facing surfaces show the systems, evaluation and measurement work only. The physics stays public and findable, not featured. This is
   curation, not hiding.
5. **Private vocabulary.** "Quniverse", "fleet", "Ignorance Audit", "Joules-per-Solution", "QNFO is not an acronym" are meaningful to you and opaque to everyone else. "Ignorance" in particular reads as a strange
   brand. Fix: each coined term gets a plain gloss the first time ("the Universal Ignorance Audit: a structured way to find what a team or model does not know"). Lead with the plain words, name second.
6. **Autonomy language works against you with employers.** "User-free", "no human in the loop" says to a hiring manager that nobody is accountable. They are hiring a person with judgment. Fix: describe the system
   as something you design, supervise and are accountable for, and name the decisions only a human took (what to measure, what to stop, what to retract).
7. **The numbers on your own ledger drift.** The claim ledger says "44+ live workers"; 39 are live today. The About page says 728 papers "counted live". Small mismatches are what a careful reader checks first.
   Fix: say "dozens of workers" in prose and let live figures come from a live page (item REACH-FOCUS-1.5).
8. **Credentials point away from the target.** Your degrees are urban planning, with no CS degree and no peer-reviewed machine-learning record. Frontier-lab research-scientist roles are therefore unlikely and a poor
   use of time. Where your record is strong (section 5) it is rare, so aim there.
9. **A gap and a shift.** 2023-24 nonprofit, 2024-now independent. It reads as drift unless the through-line explains it. The nonprofit is part of the story, not a gap: an LLM legal-navigation tool for people
   without a lawyer is applied AI for people who need it.
10. **You are a thinker, and the web rewards pitching.** That is fine. The answer is not to pitch harder, it is to publish thinking in a form that carries itself: a short, specific, checkable note with a
    number in it. Section 6 keeps you on that footing.

## 4. Message by audience and channel

Same person, different first sentence. The first sentence answers the reader's question, not yours.

| Reader | Their question | First sentence | Proof they can open | Channel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hiring manager, AI product or research ops | Can this person ship and run something real? | "I built and run an autonomous research system, 39 workers live, and here is where it fails." | case study, dashboard | LinkedIn, email |
| Researcher in AI evaluation or safety | Is this method sound and new to me? | "I found that my own most-downloaded paper's trajectory does not follow from its equations. Here is the check." | reproduction, library | LessWrong, X, email to authors |
| Research or policy leader | Can this person run a program? | "I managed a $1.5M+ federal research portfolio and led the Livability Index, cited in 20+ studies." | Livability Index, CV | LinkedIn, introductions |
| Recruiter | Does this match my role and can I place them? | Their role's keywords, then your location and availability. | LinkedIn | LinkedIn, inbound reply |
| Engineer | Does this run? | "`lake build` passes, tests pass, install works." | repositories | GitHub, Show HN |

Posting rule: **one claim, one number, one link a stranger can check.** A post that cannot meet that is not posted. This also fixes the thinker/pitchman problem: you are never asked to sell, only to show a result.

## 6. What gets you discovered: five hours a week, in this order

1. **Fix the front door once (week 1).** LinkedIn, GitHub, ORCID, X, Bluesky, Mastodon, the work-with-me page: section 3 text, the five selected works, nothing else featured. REACH-FOCUS-1.4 and 1.5 do the site.
2. **One artifact a month that the people in section 5 read.** Case study 1 is the first. Next: the Agentic Collapse correction, written for AI researchers. Specific, with a number and a check.
3. **Five named people a week, one to one.** People doing the work in section 5, not recruiters. Each message cites a specific thing they made, says one useful thing you found, and asks nothing large. The fleet prepares the
   dossier (REACH-FOCUS-1.7); you write and send. Mass outreach stays off: it breaks the consent rules and it does not work.
4. **One public venue a quarter.** Use the accepted ignorance-audit conference paper for a talk. Post the weekly pack (REACH-FOCUS-1.3) on Hacker News or LessWrong by hand.
5. **Get three outside voices.** Ask people who have run the libraries or read the work to say so publicly. Add each to the Featured section.

Recruiters: turn on LinkedIn "open to work" for recruiters only, reply within a day, and answer with their role's own keywords. Inbound is a bonus, not the plan: most senior roles are filled through people who know someone.

## 7. Measures and what would prove this wrong

- Inbound: LinkedIn profile views a week, recruiter and employer messages a week, replies to the five weekly one-to-one messages (target: 2 of 5 reply within two weeks), interviews and offers from `qnfo-jobsearch` `/api/outcomes`.
- Prediction (decision_log): `jobsearch_reply_rate_90d >= 0.1 within 28 d`, which in practice means at least 2 substantive replies from section 5 organisations within 28 days of the front door being fixed (profiles posted and 1.4 deployed). Falsified if none arrive: then the problem is the work's visibility to
  those people, not the presentation, and the next lever is the outside voices in step 5.
- Strongest argument against all of this: hiring at the top tier is a network and track-record game, and a well-edited profile does not substitute for either. This plan raises the odds of being taken seriously when
  you are looked at. It does not by itself get you looked at, which is why steps 3 to 5 exist.
