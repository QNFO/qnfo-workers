From Statements to Questions: AI as Epistemic Repair Infrastructure

Using LLMs to surface hidden assumptions, convert hardened claims into testable questions, and support epistemic repair without pretending all conflict is merely misunderstanding.
Rowan Brad Quni-Gudzinas / QNFO · remediation version 2026-09-26 · A0 landscape
“We want to see what is hidden by what we see.”

After Magritte: in conflict, the visible statement can hide the assumptions that make the statement possible.

Problem

Humans often make statements rather than ask questions. Statements close uncertainty; questions expose it. Conflict escalates when each side treats its interpretation as perception rather than hypothesis.

statementquestionrepair

Core thesis

LLMs are most useful not as arbiters of truth, but as cheap instruments for mapping what participants do not know they do not know.

Three-layer conflict model

1. Epistemic conflict

Misunderstanding, misperception, missing context, untested assumptions.

AI leverage: high — assumption extraction, steelmanning, falsifier search.

2. Interpretive conflict

Different values, narratives, identities, meanings.

AI leverage: moderate — frame translation, vocabulary bridging, repair rehearsal.

3. Structural conflict

Power, incentives, scarcity, coercion, commitment problems.

AI leverage: indirect — verification, documentation, negotiation support; not reconciliation by default.

The S→Q protocol

1. State the belief
“They are acting in bad faith.”
2. Extract hidden assumptions
What evidence distinguishes bad faith from fear, incentives, confusion, or constraint?
3. Generate the strongest alternative frame
How might they describe this conflict without lying?
4. Identify falsifiers
What observation would change my view?
5. Choose repair or boundary
Is this epistemic, interpretive, or structural conflict?

The tool cuts both ways

  • Fluent false reconciliation can hide abuse or coercion.
  • Perspective modeling can become manipulation.
  • Synthetic evidence can degrade verification and trust.
  • Sycophantic AI can validate grievance rather than test it.
  • Some conflicts require boundary, exit, enforcement, or protection.

Abstract

Human conflict often begins not with a lack of speech, but with speech that has hardened into statements. This poster proposes a framework for using large language models as tools of negative epistemology: not to decide who is right, but to surface what participants do not know they do not know. The framework distinguishes epistemic, interpretive, and structural conflict and introduces a statement-to-question protocol for repair, negotiation, boundary-setting, or enforcement.

Takeaway

Convert statements into questions. Use AI to interrogate your own framing before using it to defeat someone else’s.
