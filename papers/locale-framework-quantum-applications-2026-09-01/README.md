# Locale Framework Applied to Quantum Computing Innovations & Practical Applications

**Author:** Rowan Brad Quni-Gudzinas
**License:** CC BY-NC-SA 4.0

## About this paper

Quantum computing is usually sold through a small set of recurring claims: continuous-variable
systems are naturally advantaged, more modes mean more power, quantum advantage has been
demonstrated at device level, complex amplitudes are essential, a transmon is a qubit, and fault
tolerance is a matter of years.

This paper treats every one of those claims as a conditional truth: true inside an explicit
domain of applicability, false at its boundary. It states the eight most widely repeated claims,
makes each claim's domain explicit, and pairs each boundary with the paper that documents where
it breaks — presented at the 23rd International Conference on Quantum Physics and Logic
(QPL 2026), with one independent arXiv result. The boundaries are not defects; they are where
the physics relevant to practitioners lives. The one quantity that survives every boundary
examined is energy per solution.

The practical value is Table 1: a decision tool for an engineer, buyer, or investor. For each of
the eight claims, it states the domain where the claim holds, the boundary where it breaks, and
the paper that documents the break. What the framework itself contributes is imported from the
companion record; this paper applies it and maps the seams. The catalog is retrodictive: it is
not a prediction, and no dynamical theory of boundaries is claimed.

## For a practicing engineer

Section 3, Table 1 is the decision tool: claim by claim, where it holds, where it breaks, and the
paper that documents the break. Section 4 reads like a spec-sheet: what each paradigm is actually
benchmarked to deliver, and which roadmap numbers are conditional on unproven assumptions.
Section 5 asks what a correct answer costs, in energy per solution. Section 6 surveys the
practitioner-oriented contributions at QPL 2026 — things that can be deployed today.
